## Start with the actual reason you are replacing SOAX

A proxy provider can look great in a comparison table and still be the wrong tool for the job. Before comparing prices, identify which of these situations sounds familiar.

### You need a stable IP for an extended session

Rotating residential proxies are designed to change IPs. That is useful for broad, distributed data collection, but it can create friction when an application needs to retain the same IP over a long session.

Static ISP proxies are intended for the opposite pattern: the IP stays assigned for the subscription period. This is relevant for legitimate tasks such as:

- monitoring US search results from a consistent location;
- checking public retail pricing repeatedly;
- testing a web experience from a fixed US network identity;
- running approved data-collection jobs that need continuity rather than constant rotation;
- maintaining a consistent session in a permitted internal workflow.

If the job genuinely needs a new IP frequently, a rotating provider is still the more logical category. Buying static IPs for a rotation-heavy workload is a little like buying a refrigerator because you needed ice once.

### Your proxy spending depends too heavily on bandwidth

SOAX’s current pricing is based on monthly plan levels, credits, traffic volume, and the country tier of the IP used. Tier 1 locations cost more per GB than lower-tier countries. That structure can be sensible for low-bandwidth requests or workloads that vary a lot month to month.

The catch is that high-volume jobs can make a per-GB invoice difficult to forecast. HTML pages, images, retries, and verbose API responses all consume traffic. A job may be cheap in a small proof of concept, then become much less charming once it runs daily.

HypeProxies lists unlimited bandwidth on its public ISP plans. Its cost is based on the number of IPs and the billing term, rather than usage in GB. That makes its pricing model easier to estimate for US workloads with sustained traffic.

### You need global targeting rather than US static IPs

This is the point where an honest comparison needs to stop selling and start sorting.

SOAX advertises residential access across 195+ countries and offers country, region, city, ISP, and some more precise targeting options. If your workflow depends on collecting public data from multiple countries, testing localized experiences across continents, or rotating IPs at the request level, SOAX—or another global rotating network such as Bright Data, Oxylabs, Decodo, or IPRoyal—may be the better fit.

HypeProxies’ public ISP plans are focused on the United States. Its offering is best evaluated as a US static-ISP option, not as a like-for-like global rotating residential replacement.

> Choose the proxy type first. Provider comparisons make more sense once you know whether the job requires **rotation and geographic breadth** or **fixed US sessions and unmetered bandwidth**.

## When HypeProxies is a sensible SOAX alternative

HypeProxies is worth shortlisting when all or most of the following are true:

1. **Your target is primarily in the United States.**
   The public ISP plans are positioned around US static residential IPs. If you must reliably operate from Germany, Brazil, Japan, or a mix of countries, a global provider belongs on the shortlist.

2. **Your work benefits from an IP that does not change.**
   Static allocation can be more practical for session continuity, repeatable regional monitoring, and other approved workflows where a stable network identity matters.

3. **Traffic volume is high enough that GB metering is annoying.**
   Unlimited bandwidth is useful only if you will use it. For a light, occasional task, a low-commitment traffic plan can be cheaper. For sustained US traffic, fixed per-IP pricing is easier to budget.

4. **You can work with the available protocol support.**
   Do not assume that every proxy provider supports every protocol or tool. If SOCKS5, UDP, QUIC, or a specific integration is mandatory, confirm compatibility before paying.

   HypeProxies’ currently listed general ISP plans start at 50 IPs. It is not positioned as a one-IP trial service.

The last point matters. A lower-looking per-IP price does not help if your project needs only three endpoints. Conversely, an entry bundle can be reasonable when a team actually needs a pool of 50 dedicated static IPs.

## Other SOAX alternatives worth considering

A useful SOAX alternatives shortlist should include providers that solve different kinds of problems. The goal is not to crown a universal winner; it is to avoid buying a provider designed for a workload you do not have.

### Decodo

Decodo is commonly considered by users who want several proxy categories—residential, mobile, static residential, and datacenter options—from one dashboard. It is often a practical middle ground for teams that need geographic flexibility and a fairly approachable interface.

Consider it when you need rotating traffic and multiple proxy types, not just a stable US static allocation.

### Oxylabs

Oxylabs is often shortlisted for enterprise-oriented data-collection operations, broad proxy coverage, and specialized web-data products. It is usually more relevant when scale, compliance review, and managed tooling matter as much as the proxy endpoints themselves.

It may be more infrastructure than a small team needs, but it belongs in the comparison when global coverage and enterprise workflow support are non-negotiable.

### Bright Data

Bright Data offers a broad product range, including proxy networks and data-collection tools. It is a sensible candidate for organizations that need sophisticated location controls, diverse product types, or a larger managed-data ecosystem.

The trade-off is complexity. A powerful dashboard is great when the team needs its controls; it is unnecessary overhead when the assignment is simply “give us stable US IPs.”

### IPRoyal

IPRoyal can be useful for buyers who prioritize flexible rental durations and international ISP availability. It is particularly relevant if the static IP requirement extends beyond the United States and short-term rental options matter.

### Webshare

Webshare is often evaluated by teams that need a budget-conscious entry point, especially for simpler proxy requirements. It can be worth considering for smaller projects, but buyers should distinguish between shared and dedicated products before comparing headline prices.

A very cheap shared IP is not equivalent to a dedicated static ISP proxy. Those are different products with different operational trade-offs.

## A practical evaluation checklist before migrating

Changing providers without testing the exact workload is how teams end up writing disappointed comparison posts a month later. A short trial or small initial purchase should answer the important questions.

### 1. Confirm country and location requirements

Write down the locations you truly need—not the countries that merely sound useful. If your list is “United States only,” HypeProxies may be a focused fit. If it includes multiple continents, its public ISP range is too narrow for the full job.

### 2. Test the target with your legitimate workload

Measure success rates, latency, session persistence, and error behavior on pages or services you are authorized to access. Public performance claims can guide a shortlist, but they cannot predict every target’s policy, bot protection, traffic pattern, or tolerance.

### 3. Calculate total cost from real traffic

For SOAX, estimate monthly GB consumption by location tier. For HypeProxies, calculate the number of static endpoints actually required. Compare total monthly costs, not just $/GB or $/IP figures in isolation.

### 4. Check replacement and support terms

An IP is not useful forever just because it is static. Ask how replacements work, what availability looks like in the location you need, and which support channels are available when an endpoint needs attention.

### 5. Respect target-site rules and your own obligations

Use proxies for permitted access, authorized testing, public-data collection that complies with applicable terms, and legitimate business operations. A proxy changes the network route; it does not make disallowed activity acceptable.
